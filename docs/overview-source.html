PIXEL POLICE DEPARTMENT

Open-world, systems-driven police department sandbox inside a living pixel city  ·  working title  ·  playable prototype, September 2026
PLAY (mobile + desktop): https://jacobramirezsf.github.io/pixel-police/
SOURCE: github.com/jacobramirezsf/pixel-police  ·  local: ~/Documents/Makery/projects/PixelPolice

/ 01 / WHAT IT IS

You run a small police department inside a pixel city that does not revolve around you. Roughly 190 simulated residents commute, shop, walk dogs, drink at bars, argue, and occasionally commit crimes — whether or not you're watching. Calls come into dispatch with incomplete, sometimes wrong information. You can run everything from above — assigning units, setting policy, managing budget — or drop into any single officer and walk, drive, question, arrest, and shoot in direct control. Every decision feeds a persistent consequence system: neighborhoods remember how they're treated, city hall adjusts your funding, and gangs remember who you arrested.

"There is a little city living its life. I have a police department inside it. Calls are coming in. I can personally respond to something or manage it from above, and over time my decisions determine what kind of department and what kind of city this becomes."

Design principles: emergent stories over scripted missions · dispatch information is imperfect · civilians carry no CRIMINAL/INNOCENT labels · most calls are mundane so violence stays memorable · the player may make bad, reckless, or corrupt decisions — the game answers with consequences, never invisible walls · mobile-first controls and UI ("the city is the interface").

/ 02 / THE CITY

  • One generated district (seeded, deterministic): road grid with crosswalks and working traffic, ~80 buildings with enterable interiors (station, bank, stores, bars, houses, apartments), park, street furniture, day/night cycle with headlights and lamp glow.
  • Four neighborhoods — wealthy Harbor Heights, commercial Midtown, high-crime Old Docks, residential Cedar Park — each tracking trust, crime pressure, and tension that respond to how you police them.
  • Two gang territories: Dockside 9 and the Eastside Kings each hold a stronghold building and the blocks around it. Territory hostility (visible on the GANG TURF map overlay) rises with every member you arrest, wound, or kill — and above ~40, officers walking their turf get jumped. Entering a stronghold uninvited wakes armed defenders.
  • Civilians persist: homes, jobs, schedules, personalities, criminal records, warrants, concealed weapons, fear, and gang affiliations. They chat, panic, witness, call 911, cooperate or refuse.

/ 03 / DISPATCH & INCIDENTS

Procedural calls, ~1 every 30 seconds of play: noise, traffic stops, suspicious persons, shoplifting, fights, burglaries, robberies, armed robberies, shots fired, vehicle pursuits, gang attacks, bank robberies, shootouts, stronghold raids. What the caller reports and what is actually true diverge: weapons go unreported, unarmed people get called in as armed, and calls escalate while units travel ("UPDATE: caller now reports gunshots"). Unanswered calls expire — and cost neighborhood trust and council goodwill. Suspects decide to comply, flee, or fight based on personality, weapon, numbers, and situation. Pursuits have chase-heat (drivers yield or crash under sustained pressure), foot chases end in tackles or turned weapons, and every scene resolution — arrest, warning, citation, gone-on-arrival — is systemic, not scripted.

/ 04 / COMBAT

Free fire, fully unrestricted: draw a weapon and press-and-hold anywhere. Every round is a real ray with spread that stops on walls and trees and hits whoever is in its path — suspects, bystanders, other officers. Data-driven weapons (pistols, carbine, shotgun, taser, beanbag; criminal handguns, shotguns, rifles, knives), line-of-sight and cover, injuries from minor to critical to dead, morale-driven surrender and flight, officers who pathfind through doors to threats, EMS and coroner cleanup after scenes quiet down. Gunfire panics crowds and generates its own shots-fired calls — including against you.

/ 05 / RUNNING & GROWING THE DEPARTMENT

Consequences

  • Wrongful arrests → complaints, trust loss
  • Unjustified shootings → lawsuits (−$2,000), protests at the station
  • Injured officers hospitalized for days; the fallen get End of Watch
  • Low morale → resignations; missed calls → crime growth

City hall relationships

  • Council sets daily funding (0.6× when hostile, 1.4× when supportive)
  • Mayor unlocks grants and surplus offers
  • Both shift daily with resolved calls, arrests, missed calls, lawsuits, deaths, and citywide trust

Earning & upgrading

  • City contracts ($900–$3,800): patrol-hours in a district, crime-reduction pushes, response guarantees, stronghold raids
  • Government surplus: federal grants, $800 cruisers, free carbines, ballistic vests for the roster
  • Hire/fire officers ($500), buy cars ($3,000), buy per-officer weapons
  • SWAT team ($6,000): 4 heavy officers + van — deploy to any priority call or raid strongholds. Clearing one disbands the gang and drops district crime

The whole loop lives in one thumb-friendly panel: DEPT → OVERVIEW GROW CONTRACTS CITY HALL.

/ 06 / SANDBOX

A first-class cheats menu, not a debug console: money, god mode, infinite ammo, free equipment, heal/spawn officers and cars, free SWAT, gang heat min/max, trust min/max, consequence toggle, time skip — and one-tap incident spawning up to BANK ROBBERY and LARGE SHOOTOUT. Sandbox use flags saves but never blocks them. Normal play and chaos play are both first-class.

/ 08 / THE BUILD

Stack: Vite + TypeScript, plain Canvas 2D, DOM UI, WebAudio-synthesized SFX. Zero runtime dependencies. ~119 KB of JS (~40 KB gzipped). Deployed to GitHub Pages by CI (typecheck → build → deploy on every push to main).

Architecture invariants

  • The sim never depends on the controlled officer — direct control is input injection into one entity; everything else keeps simulating.
  • All cross-references are persistent IDs, never object pointers → saves are plain JSON; the city itself regenerates deterministically from a stored seed (mulberry32).
  • Content is data-driven: a new weapon, incident type, contract kind, or unit type is a table entry + small handlers, not a rewrite.
  • Dispatch UI never reads an incident's hidden truth — only the possibly-wrong reported text.

How it was verified

Every pass was playtested headlessly with Playwright driving the real game in Chromium (desktop + emulated iPhone): walking, driving, interacting, arrests, raids, contract payouts, save/load round-trips, long hands-off soaks — asserting on live sim state via a debug hook and screenshotting mobile/desktop/night layouts. Notable bugs this caught: a 36%-of-the-time crash from re-rolling pick() inside a find() predicate; officers combat-locking on fistfights they couldn't shoot; responders stranded by a 4-pixel vehicle-entry tolerance mismatch; and an "impossible" miss streak that turned out to be bullets legitimately stopping on a tree.
